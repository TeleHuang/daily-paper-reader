# Interaction-Stiffness-Guided Basis Allocation in Dynamic Movement Primitives for Efficient Skill Transfer 

Chan Xu, Silu Chen, Senior Member, IEEE , Dehao Wang, Xiyu Chen, Dexin Jiang, Chi Zhang, Senior Member, IEEE , Guilin Yang, Member, IEEE ,Chenguang Yang, Fellow, IEEE , and Zaojun Fang 

Abstract — Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) pro-vide a compact and stable formulation for trajectory rep-resentation and generalization in robot skill learning. How-ever, their predefined basis layout limits the allocation of approximation capacity according to stage-dependent precision requirements. To address this issue, this arti-cle proposes Stage-Criticality-Guided Dynamic Movement Primitives (SC-DMPs) with adaptive basis allocation for precision-critical skill learning. Operator-robot interaction stiffness and a trajectory-consistency cue derived from cross-demonstration task-space variability are integrated to construct a stage-criticality index. Guided by this index, basis centers are redistributed in normalized time through inverse cumulative criticality and mapped to the canonical phase domain, while their bandwidths are refined to adjust local approximation support. This enables denser and more flexible representation at high-criticality stages while re-taining sparser allocation elsewhere. Experiments on hand-writing trajectories and three real-robot tasks show that the inferred criticality is concentrated in geometrically demand-ing and task-constrained regions. Comparisons with DMPs, ProMPs, ProDMP, GP-MP, and KMP demonstrate improved trajectory reproduction, endpoint generalization, and task-critical accuracy while retaining a compact model and the stable structure of classical DMPs. 

Index Terms — Interaction stiffness, basis allocation, dy-namic movement primitives, learning from demonstration.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# LEARNING from Demonstration (LfD) has emerged as an effective paradigm for transferring dexterous human motion skills to robots, especially in settings where explicit programming is difficult or costly [1]. Representative applica-tions include fine insertion, tight-clearance assembly, and other operations with stringent precision requirements [2]. In such tasks, the robot must not only reproduce the overall shape of the demonstrated trajectory, but also maintain high geometric accuracy at critical local stages, which are often less tolerant to error than other segments [3]. Accordingly, achieving global fidelity and local accuracy at task-critical stages is central to precision-oriented trajectory learning [4]. Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) are one of the most representative trajectory-encoding frameworks in LfD, owing to their compact dynamical formulation, stability guaran-tees, and convenient spatiotemporal modulation [5]. Classical DMPs represent demonstrated motions through stable dy-namical systems, where nonlinear forcing terms encode task-specific trajectory profiles [6]. In particular, the forcing term is approximated by basis functions preset along the canonical phase variable, where the basis layout is associated with the resulting reproduction performance [7]. Although this design is generally effective, it provides limited stage-wise adaptability for precision-critical skill representation [8]. Trajectory adapt-ability and tracking accuracy can be improved by integrating DMPs with model predictive control, reinforcement learning, and adaptive or neural feedback [9]–[11]. However, such approaches may require additional environmental information or interaction, online computation, or specialized controller design, while local approximation errors introduced during trajectory encoding are not directly addressed [12]. By contrast, representation-level methods modify movement primitives to improve trajectory reproduction and general-ization, primarily through probabilistic or kernel-based for-mulations and parameter refinement. The former represents demonstrated trajectories and their uncertainty through prob-ability distributions or kernel mappings, as in ProMPs [13], ProDMP [14], GP-MP [15], and KMP [16]. However, local reproduction fidelity is typically regulated through covari-ance structures, kernel design, or hyperparameter settings, arXiv:2610.01288v1 [cs.RO] 1 Oct 2026 2 ACCEPTED FOR PUBLICATION IN IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON INDUSTRIAL INFORMATICS .

without explicitly allocating approximation support according to stage-dependent precision requirements. The latter refines movement-primitive parameterization by adjusting the number, locations, widths, or forms of basis functions, providing more explicit control over local approximation support [17], [18]. Among these refinements, basis-center redistribution relocates approximation support without increasing the basis number or altering the stable DMP dynamics, making it an efficient strategy for precision-critical skill learning [19]. Existing methods refine trajectory representations based on curvature, variance, recovered temporal structure, or mutual-information-based timestep weights [20]–[22]. These criteria characterize trajectory structure or informativeness but do not necessarily indicate task-imposed motion tolerances. For example, peg-in-hole insertion may follow a geometrically simple path yet require high positioning accuracy under tight clearances [23]. In precision-oriented physical interaction, increased stiffness may accompany stronger regulation of positional deviations, providing task information that is not evident from trajectory geometry alone [24], [25]. Robot-environment contact stiffness can reveal such constraints during sustained contact, but its force-displacement-based estimation may be unreliable in non-contact, weak-contact, or contact-transition stages [26], [27]. Operator-robot interaction stiffness can instead be estimated from muscle activation and upper-limb configuration with-out requiring robot-environment contact-force measurements, making it available across different stages of kinesthetic teach-ing [28], [29]. Existing stiffness-aware skill learning methods primarily incorporate stiffness or compliance into task execu-tion through variable-impedance profiles, compliant dynami-cal systems, or programmed interaction behaviors [30]–[32]. Alternatively, operator-robot interaction stiffness can serve as a task-informed cue for trajectory representation. Although informative, it may not fully characterize task criticality on its own, since some dynamic or periodic manipulation tasks may instead depend on compliant behavior [33], [34]. Accordingly, operator-robot interaction stiffness and cross-demonstration trajectory consistency may offer complementary cues for char-acterizing stage-dependent reproduction requirements. In this work, operator-robot interaction stiffness and atrajectory-consistency cue derived from cross-demonstration variability are integrated to construct a stage-criticality in-dex. Based on this index, Stage-Criticality-Guided Dynamic Movement Primitives (SC-DMPs) redistribute basis centers and adapt their bandwidths to allocate approximation support according to stage-dependent reproduction requirements. The main contributions are as follows. 1) A physically motivated stage-criticality index is formu-lated by integrating operator-robot interaction stiffness and cross-demonstration task-space variability, provid-ing an interpretable estimate of stage-wise reproduction-fidelity requirements. 2) A criticality-guided basis allocation strategy is devel-oped by redistributing centers through inverse cumula-tive criticality and adapting bandwidths to refine local support in high-criticality stages. 3) Comprehensive experiments on handwriting trajectories and real-robot tasks compare SC-DMPs with DMPs,

IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

The system for identifying operator-robot interaction stiff-ness is shown in Fig. 2. Before data collection, four sEMG sensors are placed over the muscle bellies of the biceps brachii, triceps brachii, brachioradialis, and flexor carpi ul-naris, parallel to the muscle fibers and away from tendons and joints. During demonstrations, the operator guides a KUKA iiwa robot through predefined tasks. Robot joint positions and shoulder, elbow, and wrist positions are recorded nominally at approximately 100 Hz, while the sEMG signals are acquired at 2000 Hz. The end-effector trajectory is reconstructed through forward kinematics, the motion-capture data are smoothed using a 50-sample moving average, and each sEMG signal is demeaned, band-pass filtered at 20-350 Hz, full-wave rectified, and low-pass filtered at 5 Hz. The processed signals are then timestamp-aligned with the robot timeline and used to estimate the interaction stiffness according to [28]. For STR-Net, we set β1 = β2 = 1 , dmodel = 64 , and the convolutional kernel size to 5. The model is trained for 300 epochs using AdamW with a learning rate of 1 × 10 −3. The loss combines a Huber-based prior-consistency term with a temporal smoothness term weighted by ζ = 1 .Moreover, five evaluation metrics are employed to assess trajectory learning and generalization performance. V. C ONCLUSION 

This article proposes SC-DMPs, which integrate operator-robot interaction stiffness and cross-demonstration trajectory variability into a stage-criticality index for adaptive basis allocation. Guided by this index, basis centers are redistributed through inverse cumulative criticality, while bandwidth refine-ment adjusts local support to provide denser representation at high-criticality stages. Experiments on handwriting trajectories and three real-robot tasks demonstrate improved trajectory reproduction, generalization, and task-critical accuracy while retaining the compact and stable DMP formulation. The current formulation targets precision-oriented skills whose critical stages are reflected in interaction stiffness and trajectory consistency. Extending it to low-stiffness critical stages associated with compliant behavior, task dynamics, or environmental context may require complementary force, vision, object-dynamics, and contextual cues.
