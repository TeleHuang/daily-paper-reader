# DreamFormer: Dream Imitation with a Transformer World Model for Language-Conditioned Robotic Manipulation 

Mostafa Kotb 1,2,∗, Cornelius Weber 1, Muhammad Burhan Hafez 3, and Stefan Wermter 1

Abstract —We introduce DreamFormer , a model-based agent that acquires language-conditioned, multi-task skills by imitating expert demonstrations within the latent imagination of a learned world model. DreamFormer first learns a task-agnostic Trans-former world model from unstructured play data, then acquires task-specific behaviors by optimizing an intrinsic reward that aligns agent-generated rollouts with expert demonstrations in latent space. Since the policy is trained on-policy inside imagi-nation, it is exposed to its own errors during training, mitigating the covariate shift inherent to offline behavioral cloning. To make long-horizon imagination affordable, DreamFormer encodes a high-resolution multi-view robotic observation into a single input token, avoiding both spatial downsampling and the multi-token representations used by prior Transformer world models. On the long-horizon CALVIN benchmark, DreamFormer outper-forms LUMOS, the comparable model-based agent, on single-environment evaluation (2.52 vs 2.34 average tasks completed per chain of five) while keeping imagination rollouts tractable. Against HULC, the behavior cloning baseline, it nearly doubles performance on zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment (1.30 vs 0.67), indicating that dynamics learned by the world model transfer more readily than a directly cloned policy. This is consistent with the role attributed to internal models in biological agents, where a model of environment dynamics supports behavior in situations not previously encountered. 

Index Terms —World models, learning in imagination, long-horizon imitation learning, language-conditioned manipulation. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# AFoundational hallmark of human and animal intelligence is the ability to construct rich internal representations of external reality—referred to as world models —that enable biological agents to mentally simulate alternative scenarios, predict future dynamics, and formulate optimal decisions prior to physical execution [1]. Inspired by this mechanism, world models [2] have emerged as a promising framework for enhancing the decision-making and predictive capabilities of embodied agents, offering a pathway toward more general and robust intelligent behavior [3]. Such models are used either to learn behavior inside their own imagination [4], [5] or to plan at runtime [6], [7]. Despite recent advances, learning world models that transfer across diverse downstream tasks remains an open challenge.

The difficulty in learning broad-purpose world models stems primarily from two methodological bottlenecks. First, in online learning paradigms, the model is continuously updated using data collected by an active policy. This creates a closed-loop exploration bias that inextricably entangles the learned transi-tion dynamics with the specific task being solved. Second, 

joint reward modeling forces the dynamics model to learn representations optimized for a singular, task-specific reward signal. This tightly couples the latent space to one objective, restricting the model’s utility for downstream policy learning or real-time planning. Consequently, these paradigms inher-ently yield task-specific world models, significantly hindering knowledge transfer across diverse tasks. We argue that constructing broad-purpose world models necessitates a shift toward offline learning from diverse offline datasets and task-agnostic (i.e., reward-free) training [8]. Once established, these models can serve as versatile foundations, used either to learn downstream task-specific behaviors or to optimize plans to reach a specified goal at runtime. Motivated by this principle, we introduce DreamFormer , a model-based agent that first learns a task-agnostic world model from unstructured play data, and subsequently acquires task-specific behaviors entirely in the imagination of the learned latent space. DreamFormer builds upon the Dream Imitation 

paradigm (Section II-A) introduced in DITTO [9], which defines an intrinsic reward encouraging the alignment between imagined policy rollouts and expert trajectories directly in the model’s latent space; optimizing this reward by reinforce-ment learning induces imitation without requiring access to expert actions. DITTO, however, validates the paradigm only in single-task settings, training a separate world model for each task without evaluating transfer across tasks. LUMOS [10] extends it to language-conditioned multi-task robotic manipulation, but adopts the DreamerV2 [4] world model in its standard configuration, which was developed for 64 × 64 

resolution inputs and therefore requires aggressive spatial downsampling of high-resolution robotic observations. At the architectural level, DreamFormer employs an autore-gressive, GPT-style Transformer [11], motivated by the proven superiority of Transformers over traditional recurrent dynamics models [7]. However, a critical bottleneck in deploying these architectures for visual control is tokenizing image observa-tions. Conventional tokenizers, such as ViT [12] or VQ-VAE [13], encode a single image into tens or hundreds of tokens. Over extended imagined rollouts, this growth in sequence length inflates both the memory footprint of the key–value cache and the cost of self-attention, which scales quadratically in the number of tokens attended over. To mitigate this, prior approaches [14], [15] have utilized a arXiv:2610.04540v1 [cs.RO] 3 Oct 2026 2

categorical variational autoencoder (VAE) [4] to encode each image into a single discrete token. However, this configuration was developed for low-resolution inputs such as Atari and does not extend to high-resolution robotics without downsampling [10]. DreamFormer alleviates this constraint by scaling the categorical VAE: replacing the shallow convolutional encoder with a ResNet-18 backbone and expanding the capacity of the categorical latent space. It encodes full-resolution multi-view observations into a single input token, so that sequence length grows only with the imagination horizon and is independent of input resolution, keeping the memory and attention cost of imagination rollouts bounded by the horizon alone. The primary contributions of this work are as follows: 1) A Broad-Purpose World Modeling Framework: We introduce DreamFormer , a model-based robotic agent that leverages offline datasets to learn a task-agnostic Transformer world model. By decoupling dynamics learning from singular reward objectives, the model builds versatile representations suitable for transfer across downstream tasks. 2) Low-Cost Long-Horizon Rollouts: We scale the cat-egorical VAE with a ResNet-18 backbone and an ex-panded latent space, encoding full-resolution multi-view observations at one Transformer token per timestep. The sequence length of an imagined rollout therefore depends only on the horizon, bounding both the key– value cache and the quadratic cost of self-attention. 3) Language-Conditioned Multi-Task Transfer: We ex-tend the Dream Imitation paradigm to train a language-conditioned multi-task policy entirely within the model’s latent space. On the CALVIN benchmark, DreamFormer 

nearly doubles the performance of the model-free base-line on zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment and outperforms the model-based baseline on single-environment evaluation. II. B ACKGROUND 

A. Dream Imitation 

Dream Imitation is an imitation learning algorithm pro-posed in DITTO [9] that leverages learned world models to reformulate offline imitation learning as online reinforcement learning within the world model latent space. First, the expert trajectories are mapped into the model latent space; then, an imitation policy is unrolled starting from an arbitrary expert state in the model latent space. Finally, an intrinsic reward rint t

is defined to encourage the alignment of the unrolled policy latent states sπt with expert latent states sEt across multiple time steps:

Furthermore, unlike standard be-havioral cloning, optimizing this intrinsic reward via reinforce-ment learning allows the agent to actively explore alternative trajectories within the simulated environment. By learning the policy ’online’ inside the world model’s imagination, Dream Imitation effectively mitigates the compounding errors and covariate shift typically associated with offline methods [16]. DITTO validates this paradigm in single-task settings, training a separate recurrent world model for each Atari game. 

DreamFormer extends it to language-conditioned robotic ma-nipulation, training a task-agnostic Transformer world model. 

B. Unstructured Play Data and Latent Planning 

Learning from Play (LfP) [17] leverages unstructured, ex-ploratory interaction data—often collected as humans freely manipulate the environment without predefined goals. Such data is diverse and unlabeled, capturing a wide range of behaviors. However, imitation learning from unstructured play data faces a multimodality problem [17], where multiple valid high-level behaviors can connect the same current state 

sc and goal state sg . To address this challenge, Play-LMP [17] introduces a planning module that encodes the diverse behaviors linking the same (sc, s g ) pair into a unified latent representation within a latent plan space. The planning module comprises two stochastic encoders: (1) a plan recognizer ,which maps an entire sequence into a latent plan, and (2) 

a plan proposer , which infers a latent plan conditioned on the current and goal states. During inference, the policy is conditioned jointly on the current state, the goal state, and the inferred latent plan to resolve behavioral ambiguity. III. R ELATED WORK 

A. Transformer World Models 

Model-based agents aim to learn a world model [2] that serves as an internal simulation of the environment’s dynamics. Then, they leverage the learned model to either train a policy inside the model’s imaginations [5] or perform real-time plan-ning with Model Predictive Control (MPC) [6]. Recently, with the success of the Transformer architecture [11] in sequence modeling across various domains [12], [18], attention has increased on using Transformers as world models. One of the earliest attempts in this direction is Trans-Dreamer [19], which replaces the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) with a Transformer-based State-Space Model (TSSM), achieving comparable performance to DreamerV2 [4]. IRIS [20] employs VQ-VAE [13] to encode visual ob-servations into 16 discrete tokens per frame, utilizing an autoregressive Transformer [11] for sequence modeling. How-ever, because self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length, applying it over a large number of input tokens leads to severe computational overhead and highly inefficient imagined rollouts during policy training. To achieve more efficient imagination, TWM [15] leverages a categorical VAE [4] to encode visual observations into a single discrete token and employs Transformer-XL [18] as its sequence model. How-ever, TWM still applies self-attention across a concatenated sequence of observation, action, and reward tokens. To further improve imagination efficiency, STORM [14] removes the reward token entirely and fuses the observation and action into a single unified token via a multilayer perceptron (MLP). 3

STORM encodes visual observations into a single discrete token similar to TWM and uses an autoregressive Transformer similar to IRIS. In a parallel effort to stabilize long-horizon rollouts, TWISTER [21] follows STORM’s architecture and supplements the training objective with Action-Conditioned Contrastive Predictive Coding (AC-CPC) to learn robust tem-poral representations directly in the latent space. Crucially, however, all the aforementioned Transformer-based agents suffer from two primary limitations. First, they predominantly rely on training task-specific world models (i.e., incorporating explicit reward modeling during training), which entangles the learned latent space with a single objective and restricts downstream policy learning strictly to that task. Second, while most of these methods (with the exception of IRIS) adopt a categorical VAE to encode visual observations into a single discrete token, this configuration was developed for low-resolution, single-view domains such as Atari games [22]. DreamFormer resolves both limitations by combining a task-agnostic world model with a scaled categorical VAE designed for high-resolution, multi-view robotics. 

B. Language-Conditioned Robotic Manipulation 

A key capability of a versatile, broad-purpose agent is the ability to execute diverse tasks specified through natural language, providing an intuitive medium for human-robot communication. Consequently, enabling robots to perform language-conditioned, vision-based multi-task learning has become a critical focus. One prominent approach is MCIL [23], which utilizes a single behavioral cloning (BC) policy conditioned on multi-context goals (e.g., a goal image or a natural language instruction). This conditioning enables MCIL to effectively leverage unstructured robot play data [17], despite the fact that only about 1% of the trajectories are explicitly annotated with language. To alleviate the inherent 

multimodality problem within unstructured play data, MCIL adopts the latent planning module (Section II-B) originally introduced in Play-LMP [17]. Motivated by these prior studies [17], [23], the CALVIN benchmark [24] provides a formal evaluation framework for language-conditioned, long-horizon, multi-task robotic manipulation. It provides a highly chal-lenging offline dataset comprising ∼24 hours of teleoperated, unstructured play data, with approximately 20K language-annotated trajectories. Building upon the foundation of MCIL, HULC [25] establishes a robust baseline on CALVIN by integrating several key innovations into a unified framework. Notable features include the use of contrastive learning to better ground language instructions in robotic actions, and the adoption of discrete categorical latent plans rather than continuous ones. Furthermore, HULC introduces a hierarchical control architecture in which a local policy, operating exclu-sively on gripper-centric observations, is conditioned on global latent plans generated by the planning module. More recently, several advanced architectures have signifi-cantly pushed the state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark. RoboFlamingo [26] adapts large, pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by attaching an explicit policy head to a frozen OpenFlamingo backbone [27]. GR-1 [28] leverages large-scale video generative pre-training to predict future frames and actions end-to-end via a GPT-style Transformer. Most importantly, all of these state-of-the-art approaches fundamentally rely on model-free behavioral cloning paradigms. To the best of our knowledge, LUMOS [10] is the only model-based baseline in this domain, applying the Dream Imitation [9] framework with a DreamerV2 [4] world model and downsamples CALVIN’s high-resolution multi-view observations to 64 × 64 before encoding. 

C. Image Tokenization in Transformer World Models 

Transformer world models simulate dynamics over a se-quence of latent tokens, and the cost of self-attention scales quadratically in the length of that sequence. The image tok-enization strategy therefore governs both the memory footprint and the attention cost of imagination rollouts. Conventional tokenizers such as Vision Transformers (ViT) [12] or VQ-VAE [13] successfully preserve spatial resolution by dividing an image into a grid of tokens (e.g., 16 or more tokens per frame), they introduce a severe sequence-length bottleneck. Over extended imagined rollouts, allocating multiple tokens per frame inflates both the key–value cache and the attention cost, as in architectures such as IRIS [20]. Recent efforts have explored various strategies to mitigate this multi-token bottleneck. For instance, CompACT [29] resamples frozen DINOv3 [30] features into 8 discrete tokens through a set of learnable queries and finite scalar quantization (FSQ) [31]. From a different angle, DINO-WM [32] utilizes frozen DINOv2 [33] features and retains the full set of ViT patch tokens. Instead of reducing the token count, it adopts a ViT-based sequence model with a frame-level causal attention mechanism, enabling the prediction of all per-frame tokens in parallel rather than generating them autoregressively token-by-token. While these are complementary optimizations—one reducing tokens per frame, the other reducing decoding steps per frame—both still allocate multiple tokens per observation. Consequently, the memory footprint and sequence length re-main heavily inflated, scaling proportionally with the rollout horizon multiplied by the per-frame token count. To bound sequence length more aggressively, another line of work [14], [15], [21] employs a categorical VAE [4] to encode observations into a single discrete token. However, standard categorical VAEs are architecturally constrained: they rely on shallow CNN encoders with a categorical latent space of 32 categories (each containing 32 classes), and are heavily optimized to operate exclusively on low-resolution images. IV. M ETHODOLOGY 

We introduce DreamFormer , a model-based robotic frame-work designed to acquire language-conditioned, multi-task policy entirely within the latent imagination of a learned world model. The training comprises two stages: First, the world model is trained in a task-agnostic fashion using an unstruc-tured play dataset. Second, an actor-critic agent is trained inside the world model’s imagined latent space via Dream Imitation [9] (see Section II-A) to synthesize a language-conditioned, multi-task policy. B. Scaling Categorical VAE 

The standard categorical VAE, initially introduced in DreamerV2 [4], encodes visual observation ot into a latent cat-egorical distribution comprising 32 categories, each containing 32 classes ( 32 × 32 ). To construct a stochastic latent state zt, a single class is sampled from each category, yielding 32 one-hot vectors, each of length 32. Flattening zt yields a sparse binary vector of dimension 1024, containing exactly 32 active bits (ones) while the remaining elements are zeros. This standard (32 × 32 ) categorical VAE is heavily optimized to operate on 

64 ×64 low-resolution, single-view observations, such as those found in Atari environments. To operate on high-resolution, multi-view robotic environments, it requires downsampling to meet the 64 × 64 input constraint, as seen in LUMOS [10]. We propose to scale the categorical VAE to directly op-erate on high-resolution observations without downsampling. First, to extract rich, fine-grained representations, we replace the conventional shallow CNN encoder with an expressive, trainable ResNet-18 backbone, further enhanced with a spatial softmax layer [34]. The spatial softmax layer reduces each feature map to its spatial expectation, explicitly encouraging the extraction of localized, keypoint-like features. This in-ductive bias is particularly suited for robotic manipulation, where precise object and gripper positions matter more than global visual textures. Second, we expand the capacity of the categorical latent space to 64 categories, each with 32 classes (64 × 32 ). The flattened stochastic latent state zt expands to a 2048-dimensional vector with 64 active bits. This internal simulation enables sample-efficient behavior learning, allow-ing the agent to be trained within the learned model without re-quiring direct interaction with the environment. D. Agent Behavior Learning 

DreamFormer trains an actor-critic agent within the latent space of the learned world by unrolling imagined trajectories starting from an arbitrary expert state and encourages the alignment of the unrolled policy states sπt with the expert states sEt . Specifically, an intrinsic reward rint is defined by Eq. 1 to measure the divergence between the agent rollouts and expert demonstrations. The actor–critic agent is trained to maximize this intrinsic reward using standard reinforcement learning. Here, the state st = {ht, z t} denotes the model state, which is composed of the hidden state ht, encoding historical information from the previous time steps (1 : t − 1) , and the stochastic state zt, capturing information from the current time step t. E. Agent Inference 

At inference (Fig. 2(b)), the policy is conditioned solely on the language instruction, while the proposal encoder infers the latent plan from the current state and latent goal. The model does not imagine at execution time: the key–value cache holds a context window C of the most recent real observations and slides forward as the episode proceeds, so it provides history to the sequence model at bounded cost. Experimental Setup 1) Benchmark: The CALVIN challenge [24] consists of four environments A, B, C, D with similar setup but vary in the desk color and the layouts of items, as shown in Fig. 3 (Left). The challenge offers a dataset of ∼24 hours of teleoperated unstructured play data with multiple sensor modalities, in which only 1% is annotated with language instructions. All methods are evaluated on the long-horizon benchmark, which consists of 1000 unique sequences of instruction chains. The agent’s goal is to sequentially solve up to five tasks using only onboard sensors, relying exclusively on language instructions to transition between tasks, as shown in Fig. 3 (Right). 2) Baselines: We compare against a range of methods, divided into two categories. 

(1) No visual pretraining: represents comparable methods to our approach where the vision encoder is trained from scratch without any pretraining phase. This category includes:

> •

GCBC [17]: Goal-conditioned behavior cloning trains the policy by maximizing the log likelihood of each action taken at each time step and does not use latent plans.  

> •

MCIL [23]: Multi-context imitation learning trains asingle behavioral cloning policy conditioned on multi-context goals and employs latent plans.  

> •

C. Imagination Rollouts Analysis 

We analyse imagination rollouts along two axes: the fidelity of the predicted states, and the computational cost of produc-ing them. These are the two sides of the trade-off—reducing the tokens per observation lowers rollout cost, but only helps if the resulting latent still supports accurate long-horizon prediction. We first examine rollout fidelity qualitatively on held-out trajectories, then compare rollout cost against other Transformer world models using the configurations reported in their respective papers. 

1) Rollout Fidelity: To assess whether encoding a multi-view robotic observation into a single input token retains enough information for long-horizon prediction, we perform open-loop rollouts on held-out validation trajectories; Fig. 4 shows an example. The world model takes the first 8 frames as context and then autoregressively predicts the remaining 56 steps, conditioned only on the ground-truth action sequence and receiving no further observations. The predicted frames are decoded from the prior ˆz, while the context frames are decoded from the posterior z.Across the full horizon, the predicted arm pose and scene layout remain closely aligned with the ground truth, and the manipulated block is tracked through the grasp, with its position drifting slightly toward the end of the sequence. The gripper view is reconstructed less sharply than the static view throughout, including within the context window; since those frames are decoded from the posterior rather than predicted, this blur reflects the reconstruction fidelity of the encoder– decoder rather than error accumulated during the rollout.

> •

Next-frame prediction: how the sequence model produces the tokens of the next frame—token by token, by iterative unmasking, or in a single pass ( Predict steps/frame ).  

> •

Context management: whether the sequence model at-tends over the full rollout horizon or over a fixed window of recent frames ( KV cache type ). As discussed in Sec. III-C, the sequence length determines the memory footprint and the quadratic cost of self-attention. A. World Model 

The world model consists of the scaled categorical VAE and the Transformer-based sequence model. The static and gripper views are processed by two independent ResNet-18 encoders trained from scratch, each followed by a spatial softmax layer [34]. Their outputs are concatenated and passed through a fully connected layer to obtain visual features, which a second fully connected layer maps to the logits of the posterior: a categorical distribution over 64 categories of 32 classes each. The sampled latent zt is flattened into a 2048-dimensional binary vector with 64 active entries, and gradients are propagated through the sample by straight-through estimation [36]. At each time step, zt and the action 

at are concatenated and mapped by a two-layer MLP to a single input token, so that each time step occupies one position in the sequence. Reconstruction is handled by two separate transposed-convolutional decoders, one per view, each taking the flattened latent zt as its only input and upsampling to the native resolution of that view ( 200 × 200 for the static camera, 

84 × 84 for the gripper camera). Both decoders are used only for the reconstruction objective and for visualization, and are not involved in imagination or policy learning. The sequence model is a GPT-style Transformer [11] with causal attention, whose output ht is mapped by a fully connected layer to the logits of the prior from which the next latent state ˆzt+1 is sampled. The world model has 60M parameters and was trained using a distributed setup of four NVIDIA Quadro RTX 6000 GPUs. The training procedure utilized a sequence length of 64 and a per-GPU batch size of 4, resulting in an effective batch size of 16. We presented DreamFormer , a model-based robotic agent that learns a task-agnostic Transformer world model from unstructured play data and acquires language-conditioned, multi-task behaviors entirely within its latent imagination. To make long-horizon imagination affordable at full resolution, we scaled the categorical VAE with a ResNet-18 backbone and an expanded categorical latent space, encoding multi-view observations at a single token per timestep. Sequence length therefore grows only with the imagination horizon and is independent of input resolution, keeping the memory and attention cost of a rollout bounded by the horizon alone. On the CALVIN benchmark, DreamFormer outperforms LUMOS, the comparable model-based agent, on single-environment evaluation, and nearly doubles the performance of the behavior cloning baseline on zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment. Notably, this level of transfer is reached with a visual representation learned from CALVIN play data alone, without initialization from externally pretrained models, suggesting that what transfers is the dynamics captured by the world model rather than the breadth of the data its encoder was trained on. Our ablations show that the scaled latent contributes more than the encoder architecture, and that the largest single factor is the policy objective: replacing the intrinsic reward with behavior cloning on the same imagined rollouts costs more than any architectural component. Several points remain for future work. Our evaluation is confined to a simulated benchmark, and whether the same representation transfers to real robots is untested. Encoding an entire multi-view observation at one token bounds how much spatial detail the latent can carry, which our qualitative rollouts suggest is felt most in small manipulated objects rather than in the arm or scene layout. Extending the approach to real-robot data, and to policies that consume gripper-centric detail at higher fidelity than the shared latent provides, are our next steps for future work. VIII.
