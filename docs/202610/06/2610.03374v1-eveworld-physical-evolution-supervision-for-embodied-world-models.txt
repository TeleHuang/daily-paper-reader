ABSTRACT
Embodied world models enable scalable simulation of embodied interactions for
robot learning. However, existing models are prone to Model Laziness, as they
focus on visual fidelity at the expense of physical reasoning and lack process-
level supervision over the temporal dynamics of manipulated objects. In this
work, we propose EVEWORLD, a physical evolution-supervision framework for
physically consistent target evolution. EVEWORLD consists of two components:
Instance-Guided Restoration (IGR) and Temporal Instance Alignment (TIA). First,
IGR promotes instance consistency through restoration supervision. Second, TIA
promotes cross-frame consistency by aligning target instances across adjacent
frames. We further introduce the Model Laziness Rate (MLR), a metric that
measures persistent violations of instance consistency in generated trajectories.
Extensive experiments on DreamGenBench, EWMBench, and PBench demonstrate
the effectiveness of EVEWORLD, notably achieving an 87.5% reduction in MLR
compared with GigaWorld-0. On the WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 leaderboard, our
model ranks 6th in JEPA Similarity and 17th overall, which further validates the
performance of our evolution supervision strategy.

INTRODUCTION
Embodied world models and related policy-learning methods are emerging as scalable predictive
engines for embodied intelligence, modeling diverse interaction dynamics to support robot learning,
planning, and simulation (Yang et al., 2024a; Bruce et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024; Jang et al., 2025;
Li et al., 2025b; Du et al., 2023; Schmidt & Jiang, 2024; Ye et al., 2025; Zitkovich et al., 2023;
Kim et al., 2025; Black et al., 2025; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025). Recent work (Huang et al.,
2025a; 2026; Guo et al., 2026; GigaWorld Team et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2025a; Pang et al., 2025;
Dou et al., 2026; Duan et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026; Bardhan et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025c; 2026;
Quevedo et al., 2026) has further advanced embodied world modeling and its evaluation through high-
fidelity generation, multiview and long-context modeling, action conditioning, persistent memory,
and task-oriented evaluation.
Recent embodied world models further strengthen structural faithfulness through explicit 4D RGB–
depth–normal prediction, geometry-aware action conditioning, and disentangled dynamics and visual
synthesis, with related physical-evaluation benchmarks (Zhen et al., 2025; DreamX Team et al.,
2026; Shan et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025a; Bansal et al., 2026; Meng et al., 2025; Qin
et al., 2025; Shang et al., 2026a; Motamed et al., 2026; Garrido et al., 2025). Nevertheless, stronger
visual and structural modeling does not necessarily ensure physically consistent target evolution: a
rollout may exhibit target-instance consistency failures, such as duplication or disappearance, and
cross-frame consistency failures, such as deformation and identity drift (Yuan et al., 2026; Foo
et al., 2026). Figure 1 shows a representative instance consistency failure after standard supervised
fine-tuning (SFT). We refer to such instance consistency failures as Model Laziness: frame-level
reconstruction can admit a shortcut that satisfies appearance or endpoint cues without maintaining
instance consistency throughout the interaction.
In this work, we propose EVEWORLD, an evolution-supervision framework that promotes physically
consistent target evolution and mitigates Model Laziness in embodied world models, as shown in
Figure 2. EVEWORLD comprises two complementary components: Instance-Guided Restoration
(IGR), which promotes instance consistency by restoring count-perturbed videos, and Temporal
Instance Alignment (TIA), which promotes cross-frame consistency by aligning target instances
across adjacent frames. To quantify Model Laziness, we introduce the Model Laziness Rate
(MLR), a metric that measures persistent violations of target-instance consistency over a rollout.
Extensive evaluations on DreamGenBench, WorldArena 2.0, EWMBench, and PBench demonstrate
the effectiveness of EVEWORLD in promoting physically consistent target evolution. Notably, on
the WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 leaderboard, EVEWORLD achieves an EWMScore-P of 70.17, ranking
17th overall and 6th in JEPA Similarity. These results further support the effectiveness of evolution
supervision under large-scale world-model evaluation.

RELATED WORK
Video Generation Models for Robotics.
Large-scale video models provide strong priors for
embodied prediction and scalable robot-data generation (Blattmann et al., 2023; Brooks et al., 2024;
Team Wan et al., 2025; Kong et al., 2024), enabling world models for robot learning, planning, and
simulation (Yang et al., 2024a; Zhou et al., 2024; Jang et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025;
GigaWorld Team et al., 2025). Recent work (Zhen et al., 2025; DreamX Team et al., 2026; Shan
et al., 2026; Fu et al., 2026) further strengthens structural modeling through richer representations
and conditioning: TesserAct jointly predicts RGB, depth, and normals, DreamX-Phi incorporates
geometric action conditioning and depth supervision, and DVG-WM disentangles dynamics from
visual synthesis, and RoboMaster improves multi-object interaction modeling. In contrast, our
EVEWORLD retains the host model’s native representation and introduces evolution supervision
during post-training to promote physically consistent target evolution.
Physical Reasoning in Embodied World Models.
Prior works (Yang et al., 2024b; Traub et al.,
2024; Jeong et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2025a; Dou et al., 2026)
improve object-centric and temporally consistent modeling through correspondence, tracking, object
permanence, and persistent memory, while other studies (Deng et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2025; Khose
et al., 2026) examine object dynamics, quantity behavior, and broader aspects of physical reasoning
from different perspectives. However, despite these advances and evaluations (Yang et al., 2024b;
Traub et al., 2024; Jeong et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2025a; Dou et al., 2026; Deng et al., 2026; Sun
et al., 2025; Khose et al., 2026), physically consistent target evolution remains challenging, with
manipulated objects exhibiting failures in both instance consistency and cross-frame consistency.
In contrast, our EVEWORLD introduces evolution supervision to explicitly promote physically
consistent target evolution.

WHY EMBODIED WORLD MODELS BECOME LAZY
3.1
PROBLEM STATEMENT
Given an initial observation I0 and an instruction c, an embodied video world model predicts future
frames x1:T . We focus on manipulation scenarios that require instance consistency, where the
target-instance count is expected to remain unchanged throughout the interaction.
Let Nt(I0, xt, c) denote the number of instances of the target specified by c, with N0(I0, c) denoting
its initial count. We define Model Laziness as
ML(I0, x1:T , c) = I[∃t ∈{1, . . . , T} : Nt(I0, xt, c)̸ = N0(I0, c)] .
(1)
This captures instance consistency failures caused by unsupported target duplication or disappearance.
Interactions that intentionally change the target-instance count, such as cutting or assembly, are
outside the scope of the current formulation and MLR protocol. Robot-induced occlusion is handled
separately through an occlusion-aware check during MLR evaluation.
3.2
CAUSES OF MODEL LAZINESS
Standard training emphasizes visual reconstruction while providing limited process-level supervision
for physically consistent target evolution (Denton & Fergus, 2018; Bengio et al., 2015; Jeong et al.,
2025). This leaves two key gaps: instance consistency and cross-frame consistency.
Missing Instance-Consistency Supervision. Experimental results show that Standard SFT retains
92%–99% of the injected target area across different perturbation settings (Appendix Figure 10,
Appendix C.2). Because the perturbed region is small relative to the full frame, its reconstruction
error can be diluted by the surrounding scene, providing insufficient supervision for recovering the
correct target-instance count. IGR (Section 4.1) addresses this gap by providing a direct restoration
target and assigning greater spatial weight to the perturbed region.
Missing Cross-frame Consistency Supervision. IGR improves instance consistency, but a correct
target-instance count alone does not ensure cross-frame consistency; the target may still become
distorted, fade, or shift abruptly across adjacent frames, as shown in Figure 2(b). This stems from

4
METHOD
Framework Overview.
Given a clean demonstration video x and its instruction c, EVEWORLD
uses IGR (Section 4.1) to construct a count-edited video ˜x and trains the model to restore x, assigning
higher weights to restoration-related regions to promote instance consistency. TIA (Section 4.2)
operates within the video diffusion transformer to align target features across adjacent frames
and promote cross-frame consistency. Together, IGR and TIA enable physically consistent target
evolution in the final video output. Notably, the demonstration video is used for training supervision;
at inference, EVEWORLD takes only the initial image and instruction as input.
4.1

EXPERIMENTS
5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND METRICS
Benchmarks and Data Splits.
We use the official DreamGen GR1 fine-tuning split, which contains
92 robot-manipulation videos paired with instructions, and primarily evaluate on DreamGenBench,
comprising 126 manipulation tasks across unseen environments, objects, and behaviors (Jang et al.,
2025). We further evaluate cross-domain generalization on WorldArena 1.0, cross-trial robustness on
PBench, cross-distribution generalization on AgiBot–EWMBench, and cross-backbone generalization
using FlowWAM on WorldArena 2.0 and held-out RoboTwin actions (Shang et al., 2026a;b; AgiBot-
World-Contributors et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2025; Mu et al., 2025; NVIDIA, 2025b; Huang et al., 2024;
Liu et al., 2024b; Unterthiner et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2026a).
Evaluation Metrics.
We evaluate instruction following with Qwen-IF and Gemini-IF (Bai et al.,
2025; Gemini Team et al., 2023); WorldArena 1.0/2.0, EWMBench, PBench, and RoboTwin assess
complementary aspects of perceptual quality, motion, semantics, consistency, and task fidelity.
Related evaluations (Deng et al., 2026; Khose et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2025), including RBench,
WFM-Eval, and video numeracy benchmarks, provide indirect signals related to object quantity
and persistence. In contrast, we propose the Model Laziness Rate (MLR), a metric that explicitly
measures target-instance consistency at the rollout level using the observed initial count N0 as its
reference, without requiring a prompt-specified count or reference future. We exclude under-counts
attributable to robot-induced occlusion while retaining all over-counts, and denote the resulting

Model and Training Setup.
For the main experiments, we initialize EVEWORLD from the 2B-
parameter GigaWorld-0 Video-Pretrain-2B (GigaWorld Team et al., 2025) and compare against
CogVideoX1.5, Wan2.2, and Cosmos-Predict2 as zero-shot baselines (Yang et al., 2025; Team
Wan et al., 2025; NVIDIA, 2025a). Standard SFT, EVEWORLD, and all ablations share the same
initialization, training data, and 250-step optimization budget. We fine-tune all transformer parameters
on eight NVIDIA H20Z GPUs with an effective batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 4.32 × 10−5.
For cross-distribution and cross-backbone experiments, we apply the same evolution-supervision
method to the corresponding data and backbone. Details are provided in the appendix.
5.2
MAIN RESULTS
Overall Results.
As shown in Table 1, EVEWORLD substantially mitigates Model Laziness while
improving instruction following. Compared with Standard SFT, EVEWORLD reduces MLR from
11.11% to 1.59% (85.7% relative reduction), while Qwen-IF increases from 73.81% to 80.16%
and Gemini-IF from 53.57% to 60.85%. Appendix A (Appendix Figure 8) further shows that the
remaining violations under EVEWORLD are transient, indicating that evolution supervision improves
physical consistency without sacrificing instruction following.
Fine-grained Analysis.
Table 2 reports instruction-following performance across the Environment,
Object, and Behavior splits. Compared with Standard SFT, EVEWORLD improves Qwen-IF on
all three splits, with the largest gain on Behavior (68.09% to 76.60%), while Gemini-IF improves
notably on Environment (51.72% to 72.41%) and Object (42.00% to 50.33%). Overall, these results
show that evolution supervision yields broad gains across different generalization dimensions.

GENERALIZATION EVALUATION
Zero-Shot Domain Generalization.
On WorldArena 1.0 (Shang et al., 2026a), which includes
unseen scenes and robot embodiments, EVEWORLD improves EWMScore-local-8 from 53.95
to 56.76 (+5.21%) over Standard SFT while reducing MLR from 27.39% to 13.38% (−51.15%)
(Table 3). These results demonstrate improved cross-domain generalization in both motion quality
and target-instance consistency.
Generalization Across Training Distributions.
On EWMBench (Hu et al., 2025), EVEWORLD
trained on the same AgiBot data (AgiBot-World-Contributors et al., 2025) and optimization budget
as Standard SFT improves Motion and Overall by 3.48% and 1.24%, respectively (Table 4). These
results show that the gains of EVEWORLD extend beyond the DreamGen training distribution,
particularly in motion modeling.

methodological difference from SFT250. As shown in Table 8, EVEWORLD improves all six paired

consistent robustness across inference seeds and rollout horizons.

6
CONCLUSION
We presented EVEWORLD, a physical evolution supervision framework for embodied world models,
and proposed the Model Laziness Rate (MLR), a metric to quantify target-instance consistency
failures, which we term Model Laziness. EVEWORLD combines IGR for instance consistency and
TIA for cross-frame consistency to promote physically consistent target evolution. Experiments show
an 85.7% MLR reduction on DreamGenBench with improved instruction following, 17th overall and
6th in JEPA Similarity on the WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 leaderboard, and generalization gains across
training distributions and model backbones.
Limitations and Future Works.
The limitations of our current formulation mainly arise from its
local, pairwise supervision, which does not explicitly capture long-horizon state dependencies or
complex multi-object interactions. Future work will further expand the EVEWorld framework with
broader forms of evolution supervision for longer-horizon and relational dynamics.

Positioning and Design Advantages of MLR.
Taken together, these evaluations address comple-
mentary aspects of video and embodied-world-model quality but rely on different evaluation anchors.
RBench embeds appearance and disappearance within broader physical and task-level assessment;
WFM-Eval evaluates hallucination events relative to a ground-truth future; T2V-CompBench evaluates
quantities explicitly specified by the prompt; and RoboWM-Bench assesses downstream embodied

execution. MLR is designed for the complementary regime in which the conditioning observation
itself defines the expected target count and no reference future is assumed. It therefore provides
four properties that are particularly useful for embodied manipulation evaluation: it is initial-state
anchored, because the observed initial target count N0 is obtained directly from the conditioning
state; reference-free, because no ground-truth future is required; rollout-level and persistence-aware,
because sustained violations are distinguished from isolated frame-level fluctuations; and occlusion-
aware, because robot-supported under-counts are explicitly exempted. Together, these properties
make MLR a dedicated diagnostic for persistent target-instance consistency failures in embodied
world-model rollouts.

A.2
SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS OF MLR
MLR is designed for manipulation scenarios that require instance consistency, including grasping,
transport, placement, and rearrangement, where the observed initial target count N0 is expected to
remain unchanged throughout the interaction. The protocol does not assume N0 = 1 and therefore
applies to scenes containing multiple instances of the manipulated target.

Interactions that intentionally change the target-instance count, such as cutting, splitting, assembly,
or disassembly, fall outside the current formulation because the expected target count can change as
part of successful task execution. Evaluating such tasks would require an instruction-conditioned
expected count rather than a fixed initial-state reference.
MLR is also deliberately specific to target-instance consistency. Count-preserving failures, such as
identity drift, deformation, or other changes that preserve the number of detected target instances, are
not captured by MLR. These failures constitute a complementary form of temporal inconsistency and
motivate the cross-frame supervision introduced by TIA.

A.6
OPTIONAL VLM EVALUATION
For settings where a capable multimodal model is available, VLM-based temporal reasoning can
provide an optional review of detector-based MLR events. This review is auxiliary: it neither
replaces nor modifies the frozen detector-based MLR protocol, nor does it change the MLR labels
or values reported in this paper. Because VLMs differ in visual and temporal reasoning capability,
their judgments are treated as supporting evidence rather than ground truth, with manual review for
ambiguous cases. Our qualitative distortion analysis uses the same framework with additional manual
verification; examples are provided in Appendix F.3.
VLM Inputs and Outputs.
When reviewing the same evaluation set, the eligible set D+, target
query q(c), and initial target count N0 are inherited from the frozen primary MLR protocol. The
VLM can be provided with the conditioning frame, instruction, target query, N0, frames aligned with
the MLR sampling timeline, and a denser temporal view of the rollout. A JSON schema can constrain
the output, including event type, interval, supporting frames, visibility explanation, and confidence.
MLR Judgment.
For each candidate event, the VLM reviews the temporal evidence and returns
the structured judgment above. Duplication and disappearance are mapped back to the fixed MLR
sampling timeline and counted only when they satisfy the original persistence rule. Benign visibility
loss, including supported robot or non-robot occlusion, temporary out-of-view motion, cropping, or
other temporally supported visibility changes, is flagged as potential false positives of the primary
detector-based MLR protocol and reported separately; they do not alter the primary MLR values.
Distortion is excluded from MLR because the target count is preserved, but retained as a separate
cross-frame consistency failure. Uncertain cases can be passed to manual review, with repeated
judgments or a refutation query optionally used to check decision stability.

B.3
JOINT TRAINING AND INFERENCE
The full training objective combines the IGR restoration loss with the TIA consistency loss:
L = LIGR + λTIA LTIA.
(21)
For samples with an applied IGR corruption, LIGR restores the clean latent from the count-perturbed
input; otherwise, it reduces to the base clean-video training objective. The consistency term is
computed on the post-transport features. Its weight and optional noise gate are set per backbone in
Appendix C.1.
At inference, the model receives the first frame, the instruction, and the benchmark-specific control
input; the parser, the grounder, and the corruption generator are training-time only.

TRAINING AND LONG-HORIZON BEHAVIOR
Training dynamics.
We first examine how instance consistency and instruction following evolve
during post-training. As training proceeds toward the selected step-250 checkpoint, MLR generally
decreases while Qwen-IF improves, indicating that the reduction in Model Laziness is accompanied
by better task fidelity rather than obtained at its expense.
Guidance sensitivity.
We further evaluate classifier-free guidance (CFG) (Ho & Salimans, 2022)
across multiple post-training checkpoints and guidance weights. Table 22 shows that stronger guid-
ance generally raises Gemini-IF, but MLR is substantially less monotonic. For example, increasing
w can improve instruction following while leaving MLR unchanged or even increasing it at the
same checkpoint. Conversely, the lowest MLR values occur only for particular combinations of
training stage and guidance weight rather than systematically at the strongest guidance setting. These
results show that CFG primarily controls inference-time instruction fidelity, whereas target-instance
consistency remains dependent on the learned post-training behavior.
Long-horizon behavior.
Finally, we examine whether longer rollouts introduce broader physical
and process-level degradation. On PBench (NVIDIA, 2025b), overall physical-QA performance
decreases as the output duration grows from 3.8 s to 19.8 s, with temporal reasoning degrading
most strongly (Table 23). Averaged over all five durations, Standard SFT improves all four PBench
dimensions over the pretrained backbone. The advantage becomes more pronounced at longer
horizons: over durations of at least 9.8 s, it improves Domain, Phys, and Time by 1.65, 1.95, and 4.24
points on average, respectively. These results indicate that post-training improves broader physical
reasoning even as long-horizon generation remains increasingly challenging.

D.1
PHYSICSLATENT
PhysicsLatent augments the conditioning sequence with eight learned process tokens. The tokens
are computed from the pooled reference-frame latent and prompt embedding and are read by every
cross-attention layer. Weak-label heads predict the target’s start and goal boxes, contact events, a
coarse 2D trajectory, and the manipulation phase, using pseudo-labels parsed from the instruction
together with color and motion heuristics. Against the pretrained 5.8 s control (92 matched pairs),
MLR moves from 17.4% to 16.2% while Gemini-IF drops from 45.65 to 40.22, leaving no joint gain.

D.4
CAUSAL FRONTIER
Causal Frontier generates a video autoregressively in blocks of four latent frames. At each step, the
model sees only the clean latents of previously committed blocks, which a near-zero noise level and
a mask channel mark as history. The denoising loss applies only to the active block. Generation
commits each block irreversibly before denoising the next. At the current budget, prediction errors
compound over the rollout (Bengio et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025b): against
control50 (16 matched pairs), MLR rises from 30.8% to 41.7% and Gemini-IF falls from 27.08 to
12.50.

Algorithm 7 Causal Frontier training and generation. Future blocks never enter the input;
generation commits each block irreversibly.

refinement pass.

RELATED WORK
We group related work into structured embodied world models and physical alignment, object editing,
and approaches based on correspondence, tracking, or memory.
Structured embodied world models and physical alignment.
• 4D scene representations. TesserAct extends video generation to jointly predict RGB, depth,
and surface normals for 4D scene reconstruction (Zhen et al., 2025). While its richer outputs
improve geometric modeling, EVEWORLD retains the host model’s native representation and

• Disentangled dynamics. DVG-WM separates a low-resolution dynamics preview from high-
fidelity video refinement and evaluates whether the robot acts on the intended object (Shan
et al., 2026). Correct target selection, however, does not ensure that the target remains a single
consistent instance throughout the interaction; IGR explicitly trains recovery from count-perturbed
demonstrations.
• Object-aware training. DreamX-Phi combines action conditioning and depth prediction with
target-mask-weighted reconstruction and relational supervision from a frozen V-JEPA teacher
(DreamX Team et al., 2026). TIA adds layer-selected local matching and feature transport for the
manipulated target across adjacent frames, while IGR supplies an explicit restoration target when
instance consistency is violated.
• Physics-preference learning. ABot-PhysWorld uses vision-language discriminators to select
preferences for physically plausible interaction videos, including contact behavior (Chen et al.,
2026b). Its video-level preference signal does not explicitly pair a duplicated target with a clean
future; IGR uses that pairing to directly supervise instance-consistent target evolution.
Object editing and corrective supervision.
• Mask-free video editing. LoVoRA uses learnable localization to add or remove objects in a
supplied video from text, without an external mask at inference (Xiao et al., 2025b). IGR instead
uses object insertion as a training perturbation; EVEWORLD predicts the future from an initial
image and instruction without an edit request identifying a spurious target.
• Effect-aware removal. ROSE removes a specified object and its effects on the scene, including
shadows and reflections, from an existing video (Miao et al., 2025). IGR uses the inserted target
for a different purpose: restoring the clean manipulation demonstration during post-training, so
the world model learns instance consistency before generating future interactions.
Correspondence, tracking, and persistent memory.
• Joint generation and tracking. Track4Gen jointly learns video generation and point tracking to
reduce appearance drift (Jeong et al., 2025). TIA instead selects a layer using target-matching
error and locally matches and transports the manipulated target’s features between adjacent
frames, directly supervising its cross-frame consistency during generation.
• Geometry-distilled correspondence. GEM-4D distills dense 4D correspondence from a geome-
try model into the video backbone, improving geometric consistency without an auxiliary branch
at inference (Zhou et al., 2026). TIA focuses its matching and transport on the manipulated target,
while IGR adds restoration supervision for instance errors that geometric correspondence alone
does not explicitly correct.
• Scene-revisitation memory. WorldMem stores observations indexed by poses and timestamps to
preserve spatial and temporal consistency when a simulated agent revisits a scene (Xiao et al.,
2025a). Our evolution supervision instead addresses errors in how the manipulated target changes
during an interaction, including unsupported duplication and cross-frame drift.
• Object-centric memory. SlotMemory organizes key-value features around reusable object slots,
improving long-video entity persistence and showing qualitative object-count stability (Dou et al.,
2026). IGR complements such persistence by explicitly restoring count-perturbed manipulation
videos to supervise instance consistency, rather than relying on stored entity features alone.
