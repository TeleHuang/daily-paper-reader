ABSTRACT 

In this work, we aim to develop an embodied MLLM agent that understands and solves embodied tasks by learning, abstracting, and reusing transferrable and gen-eralizable behavioral patterns. To this end, we propose StateSkill, a new skill rep-resentation for embodied agents, which introduces the concept of “finite-state ma-chines” into skill modeling for effective and efficient skill construction and learn-ing. The core idea of StateSkill is to model embodied interaction experiences as a finite-state machine, where a skill is organized around a set of task-progress states, state transitions, and state-conditioned guidance, enabling embodied agents to dy-namically adjust their skills as the task progresses while keeping skill contexts compact and stage-relevant. We further develop StateSkill-Evolve, an experience-driven evolution mechanism that iteratively refines StateSkill through retrospec-tive analysis of new successful and failed state-guided trajectories. Upon this, we introduce StateGRPO, which integrates StateSkill evolution and model optimiza-tion via state-conditioned GRPO into a unified iterative learning loop, enabling state-conditioned skills and model capabilities to co-evolve and reinforce each other. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed meth-ods and trained model (i.e., StateSkill-Agent) across various embodied tasks. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Embodied models like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently emerged as a promis-ing paradigm for building general-purpose agents for embodied AI by mapping visual observations and language instructions directly to robot actions (Zitkovich et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024). De-spite rapid progress driven by large-scale collections of robot demonstrations (Open X-Embodiment Collaboration, 2024; Ghosh et al., 2024), current VLA methods remain highly dependent on the cov-erage and diversity of available trajectories, and their performance can degrade substantially when demonstrations are limited (Lin et al., 2025). A key reason lies in that these methods predominantly learn by directly imitating task-specific demonstrations, with few explicit mechanisms for identify-ing, abstracting, and reusing behavioral regularities shared across demonstrations of different tasks as in Figure 1 (a). Consequently, the resulting models may overfit to training tasks, leading to limited transferability and generalization, particularly in data-scarce scenarios. In this work, we aim to develop an embodied MLLM agent that understands and solves embodied tasks by learning, abstracting, and reusing transferrable and generalizable behavioral patterns. Re-cent advances in NLP, such as Anthropic’s Claude (Anthropic, 2025), have shown strong potential for enabling large language models (LLMs) to abstract past experiences into compact behavioral knowledge and utilize such knowledge to solve new tasks, substantially improving experience trans-fer and generalization (Wang et al., 2024a; Zhao et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2026). A core design under-lying these advances lies in the concept of “skill”: it summarizes generalizable behavioral patterns from successful or failed trajectories, capturing what actions are effective, under what conditions they should be applied, and what mistakes should be avoided, which together can subsequently guide the LLM in solving relevant but unseen tasks (Xia et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026).

An intuitive idea is to directly apply these LLM skill methods to summarize and reuse behavioral knowledge for embodied MLLM agents, which, however, does not perform well. As illustrated in Figure 1 (b), we attribute this limitation primarily to two key observed challenges. (1) Skill Repre-sentation: Most existing LLM skills are typically represented in textual form while embodied agents are required to perceive, understand and make long-horizon decisions in complex and interactive physical environments, where such text-based skills are often insufficient to represent the complex behavioral experiences and task states arising from agent-environment interactions (e.g., spatial re-lationships, agent states, and environment states), thereby leading to limited performance. (2) Skill Efficiency: Most existing LLM skill paradigms commonly load an entire skill description at once. For embodied tasks involving multiple execution stages and repeated physical interactions, this can introduce excessively long contexts containing substantial redundant or stage-irrelevant informa-tion, which increase computational overhead and may introduce distracting or conflicting guidance, particularly for embodied multimodal agents with limited long-context understanding capabilities. To tackle these challenges, we propose StateSkill, a new skill representation for embodied agents, which introduces the concept of “finite-state machines” into skill modeling for effective and efficient skill construction and learning as illustrated in Figure 1 (c). The core idea of StateSkill is to model embodied interaction experiences as a finite-state machine, where a skill is organized around a set of task-progress states, state transitions, and state-conditioned guidance. Specifically, StateSkill first decomposes embodied trajectories of similar tasks into multiple progress stages, from which it abstracts a set of progress states and their associated state transitions, and further summarizes state-conditioned skills. Each state is equipped with (a) state-conditioned language guidance de-scribing how to accomplish the current stage or subtasks, (b) a state transition gate specifying when and how the agent should transition to next states, and (c) state-conditioned action priors modeled from embodied trajectories to guide action prediction. By explicitly conditioning skills on progress states, StateSkill enables an embodied agent to track the continuously changing states of the agent, environment, and task, determine state transitions based on the current state and newly observed information, and dynamically adjust its skills in a state-conditioned manner as the task progresses. In this way, StateSkill enables effective and efficient skill construction for embodied agents. (1) The finite-state representation enables StateSkill to organize embodied interaction experiences into explicit task-progress states and transitions, equipping each state with state-conditioned language 2Preprint guidance, a state transition gate and trajectory-derived action priors. Thus, it enables skills to rep-resent complex behavioral experiences and task states arising from agent-environment interactions in a structured, state-conditioned manner, allowing embodied agents to perceive, understand and make long-horizon decisions effectively in complex and interactive physical environments. (2) The state-conditioned guidance and state transition gates enable StateSkill to load the language guid-ance associated with the current progress state and dynamically adjust it as the task progresses, with transitions determined by the current state and newly observed information. This effectively keeps skill contexts compact and stage-relevant, reducing redundant information and the risk of distract-ing or conflicting guidance and thus lowering computational overhead and easing the long-context processing burden on embodied multimodal agents. Furthermore, we extend StateSkill with an iterative experience-driven evolution mechanism, termed StateSkill-Evolve, enabling it to continuously evolve by learning from new state-guided experiences beyond its initial construction from pre-generated trajectories. StateSkill-Evolve retrospectively analyzes new successful and failed trajectories, identifies strengths and diagnoses weaknesses in the current StateSkill, and revises its state-conditioned guidance, state transition gates, and action priors. In addition, we introduce StateGRPO, which integrates StateSkill evolution and model optimization into a unified iterative learning loop. At each iteration, the current StateSkill guides the model to generate embodied trajectories with state-conditioned guidance, transition control, and action priors, while the generated trajectories are then used to optimize the model via state-conditioned GRPO. The updated model then generates new embodied trajectories with enhanced capabilities, which in turn provide fresh experience and feedback for further StateSkill evolution. By alternating between skill evolution and model optimization, StateSkill and the model progressively co-evolve, enabling structured behavioral knowledge and learned model capabilities to mutually reinforce one another. With StateSkill, StateSkill-Evolve and StateGRPO, we train our model, StateSkill-Agent. The main contributions of this work are fourfold. First, we introduce the concept of finite-state ma-chines into skill modeling and learning for embodied agents and propose StateSkill, which structures reusable behavioral knowledge into state-conditioned skills for effective and efficient embodied ex-ecution. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that explores finite-state machines for skill modeling and learning in embodied MLLM agent. Second, we develop StateSkill-Evolve, an experience-driven evolution mechanism that iteratively refines StateSkill through retrospective analysis of new state-guided trajectories. Third, we introduce StateGRPO, which unifies StateSkill evolution and model optimization via state-conditioned GRPO in an iterative loop, enabling skills and model capabilities to co-evolve. Fourth, extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of our proposed methods and trained model (i.e., StateSkill-Agent) on various benchmarks. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 EMBODIED MODEL 

Embodied models aim to enable agents to perceive environments and execute physical actions based on observations and instructions (Brohan et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2025; Driess et al., 2023; ACE-Brain Team, 2026). In recent years, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have significantly improved the generalization of embodied agents across diverse tasks by jointly modeling vision, language, and actions (Zitkovich et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2025b; Jiang et al., 2023). Beyond standard VLA modeling, recent studies have further explored world models and agentic frameworks for enhancing long-horizon decision making (Liang et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2026). For example, CaP-X (Fu et al., 2026) composes robotic manipulation through code generation, Harness VLA (Zhang et al., 2026d) employs a high-level agent to dynamically orchestrate VLA policies and control prim-itives, while ASPIRE (Lu et al., 2026) constructs and updates robotic skills through interaction. Unlike these works that mainly focus on model architectures, policy orchestration, or skill invo-cation, we focus on the representation and learning of embodied skills themselves, and propose StateSkill, a new skill representation that models embodied interaction experiences as a finite-state machine, enabling skills to dynamically adapt to task progress and environment states. 3Preprint StateSkill Construction

LLM skills abstract historical interaction experiences into reusable behavioral knowledge, allowing agents to transfer previously acquired experience to new tasks (Wu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; 2025b; Jiang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a). Specifically, Voyager (Wang et al., 2024a) stores successfully executed programs in a retrievable skill library, ExpeL (Zhao et al., 2024) summarizes reusable linguistic experience from historical trajectories, while recent works such as SkillRL (Xia et al., 2026) and SkillGen (Ma et al., 2026) further explore automatically constructing and contin-uously updating skills from successful and failed trajectories. Existing LLM skills are typically represented as textual or programmatic task-level knowledge, with limited explicit modeling of con-tinuously changing task states during execution. In contrast, StateSkill models a skill as a finite-state machine consisting of task-progress states, state transitions, and state-conditioned guidance, enabling skills to dynamically adapt to changing task and environment states throughout embodied task execution. 2.3 REINFORCEMENT LEARNING IN LLM A GENT 

Reinforcement learning enables LLM agents to directly optimize their decision-making policies through environmental interaction and reward feedback, and has recently been widely adopted for reasoning, tool use, and multi-turn agent tasks (Guo et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a; Jin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a; Feng et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026). Methods such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) op-timize model policies based on group-relative rewards. RL has also been applied to interactive agent settings through WebRL (Qi et al., 2025), ToolRL (Qian et al., 2025), and Agent-R1 (Cheng et al., 2025). Related RL methods mainly optimize model parameters using task outcomes or trajectory-level feedback (Wei et al., 2025; Kong et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025b), while the internal state structure of long-horizon tasks remains less explicitly exploited. Different from existing RL methods for LLM agents, we propose StateGRPO, which introduces task states and state-level feedback into policy optimization, while using newly collected interaction trajectories to further evolve StateSkill, enabling the joint optimization of skill knowledge and model capabilities. 4Preprint 

3 METHODOLOGY 

3.1 STATE SKILL -A GENT FRAMEWORK 

As illustrated in Figure 2, StateSkill-Agent uses task instructions and current visual observations to decompose tasks into subtasks and retrieve corresponding reusable StateSkills. It tracks execution progress through the explicit states and transitions of each active skill. Language guidance is in-troduced progressively as execution advances through these states, without loading the entire skill description upfront. After each interaction, a skill-specific gate interprets observations, executor feedback, and retained facts to update the active state or return a local skill outcome. StateSkill-Agent starts with StateSkills constructed by abstracting shared behavioral patterns from embodied trajectories. Building on these initial skills and the interaction trajectories generated dur-ing execution, StateSkill-Evolve refines skill structure and guidance using new successful and failed state-guided trajectories, while StateGRPO optimizes StateSkill-Agent’s underlying MLLM with state-conditioned feedback. The updated agent then generates further interaction experience for skill refinement, forming an iterative cycle of skill evolution and model optimization. 3.2 STATE SKILL : M ODELING EMBODIED SKILLS AS FINITE -S TATE MACHINES

5 CONCLUSION 

This paper presents StateSkill, a new skill representation for embodied agents, which introduces the concept of “finite-state machines” into skill modeling for effective and efficient skill construction and learning. The core idea of StateSkill is to model embodied interaction experiences as a finite-state machine, where a skill is organized around a set of task-progress states, state transitions, and state-conditioned guidance. By explicitly conditioning skills on progress states, StateSkill enables an embodied agent to track the continuously changing states of the agent, environment, and task and dynamically adjust its skills in a state-conditioned manner as the task progresses, while keep-ing skill contexts compact and stage-relevant. Furthermore, we extend StateSkill with an iterative experience-driven evolution mechanism, termed StateSkill-Evolve, which retrospectively analyzes new successful and failed trajectories and refines the state-conditioned guidance, state transition gates, and action priors. In addition, we introduce StateGRPO, which integrates StateSkill evolution and model optimization into a unified iterative learning loop. With StateSkill, StateSkill-Evolve, and StateGRPO, we train our model, StateSkill-Agent.
